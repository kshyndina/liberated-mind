The values session

The VLQ plus all three starter values exercises, walked through end to end. Notebook and pen recommended; you can also type into the boxes here (autosaved to your browser only). Do it alone, where nobody sees the page: honesty needs privacy.

Why writing, not picking from a list The book deliberately gives no menu of values to choose from: research it cites found that writing about a value changes behavior and health more than selecting from a list. What you get instead is 12 life areas to locate the work, and a vocabulary of action-qualities to name what you find.

Step 1 · VLQ: find your work area (~10 min)

Take the interactive VLQ, or in the notebook: list the 12 areas, rate each for importance (1–10) and for how consistently last week's actions matched your values there (1–10; anchor 10 to the best realistically achievable, and rate your own view, not others'). Your work area: importance 9–10, action 6 or less. If several qualify, pick one. If your average score lands under the mean of 61, that is a starting point and not a verdict; meet any self-attack with a defusion move.

Step 2 · Values writing — Написание ценностей (10 min timed)

Write continuously about the value living in your chosen area. Work through these questions:

  • What matters to me in this area?
  • What do I want to do so my actions reflect it?
  • When in my life was this value especially alive?
  • What have I seen when others honored it, or violated it?
  • What can I do to express it more?
  • When did I betray it, and what did that cost me?

Rules: qualities of your own actions, not "shoulds", not approval, not a wish list of what you want from life or people. Stuck? Rewrite what you already have until something new appears. You cannot do this wrong.

Step 3 · Mine the text (~5 min)

Read what you wrote. Underline concrete actions: real behaviors you could do. Circle qualities of action, the how: honestly, with love, creatively, with curiosity, compassionately, respectfully, openly, joyfully, adventurously, thoughtfully, fairly, supportively, peacefully, with humor, simply, reliably. Your own words count; that list only shows the shape.

Step 4 · Draw the sweetness — Рисуем сладость (~10 min, paper only)

  1. Recall one specifically sweet moment in this area: fully alive, connected, in flow, supported, strong. Who was there, what were you doing, feeling, thinking, how present were you? Relive it.
  2. Hold the question without words: what does this say about how I want to live?
  3. Draw whatever comes. No words on the drawing, no art judgment.
  4. Look at it. If words resonate now, write a few under the picture.
  5. Nail it to the wall: put the drawing or its photo where you'll see it daily.

Step 5 · Pain into purpose — Превращение боли в цель (~15 min)

  1. List the painful thoughts, feelings, memories, urges or sensations that block this area.
  2. Flip each one. Probe: what would I have to stop caring about for this to stop hurting? That is the value inside the pain.
  3. For each barrier, complete: "If in [situation] I [feel / think / remember...], let it remind me that what matters to me is [value]."

This will not remove the pain; it recruits the pain as a reminder of what you stand for.

Step 6 · Aftercare and the bridge to action

  • Expect a few more emotional, irritable or anxious days; values work touches what you care about, and we hurt where we care.
  • Rumination or self-flagellation → run defusion, self or presence exercises.
  • Resistance or procrastination → run acceptance.
  • Within a day or two, take the 2–3 actions from Step 3 into pivot 6: make each one SMART, start with the smallest reliable step, attach it to an existing daily routine.
