Self-tests from the book

Where do you stand right now?

Two questionnaires the book uses to point your practice: cognitive fusion (chapter 9) and valued living (chapter 13). Answers stay in your browser. Do them alone and honestly; there are no grades here.

Cognitive Fusion Questionnaire (CFQ)

Rate how true each statement is for you, from 1 (never true) to 7 (always true). The themes below follow the book's seven items in condensed form.

Answer all 7 to see your score.

Valued Living Questionnaire (VLQ)

Two ratings per life area, both 1–10. Importance: how much this area matters to you personally (not every area has to matter). Last week: how consistently your actions matched your values in this area over the past seven days. Anchor 10 to the best realistically achievable, not your ideal; rate your own view, not what others would say.

How to read it Work areas are rows where importance is 9–10 but last week scored 6 or less; the highlighted rows above. Start your values work in one of them (the guided session takes it from there). The overall score is the average of the products; wide testing put the mean at 61. A lower number is a starting point, not a verdict: if self-criticism kicks in, run a defusion exercise. And if most areas came out unimportant, check whether you were honest; the book notes that broad indifference across areas is itself associated with psychological distress.
