The playground

Four of the book's techniques you do in real time, out loud, at human speed. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

🍃 Leaves on a stream

How it works: sit comfortably, breathe once, and watch the water. Each time a thought shows up in your head, type it and press Enter: it lands on a leaf and takes about half a minute to cross. Your only job is to watch it go. Don't speed it up, don't hold it back, don't argue with it. Then wait for the next thought. Three minutes is a session.

Notice the next thought that shows up. Type it.

🚌 Passengers on the bus

How it works: first tell the bus where it's going: something you actually care about. Then board your passengers: the worries, memories and thoughts that ride along. Every so often one of them will stand up and yell at you. You get the same choice you get in real life: pull over to argue with them, or keep driving. Try both and watch the odometer.

🫧 Thought-o-matic

How it works: the book's "see it as an object", in its real three-round shape. Round 1: build the thought. Round 2: set it aside and build your reaction to it. Round 3: rebuild the original as it feels now, and compare it with round 1. It usually comes back smaller.

1 · The thought 2 · Your reaction to it 3 · The thought, revisited
Type the thought that's bothering you, then answer with the controls: if it had a size, how big would it be? A shape? A color? A speed? A texture? Take a few seconds per question; actually look at it between changes.

🫠 Word melter

How it works: the rapid-repetition technique (the book aims it at self-labels like "lazy" or "bad parent"; one word works best). You will say the word out loud once per flash. It starts at one per second and speeds up to about three per second, which is as fast as a mouth goes. Forty seconds total, then it stops on its own. If your tongue trips, good: that's the meaning coming loose. Do it somewhere you can talk to yourself.
