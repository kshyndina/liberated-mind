Interactive practice

The playground

Four of the book's metaphors as things you can do, not just read. Nothing you type leaves your browser. These are serious techniques wearing play clothes; the book insists the humor is part of the method.

🍃 Leaves on a stream

The classic defusion meditation. Type a thought that keeps circling, put it on a leaf, and let the stream do what streams do. Don't push the leaves along and don't hold them back; both are the same mistake.

When a leaf hooks you and you drift downstream after it: that's not failure, that's the exercise. Notice it happened and put the next thought on the next leaf.

🚌 Passengers on the bus

You drive the bus called Your Life. Memories, sensations and thoughts are passengers. Stopping to throw them off means going nowhere; arguing with them means letting go of the wheel. Board yours and keep driving.

Notice: the bus never stopped once while they boarded. You choose the route; they just ride. The book's paper version: write your rowdiest passengers on cards, carry them for a week, pat the pocket to welcome them aboard.

🫧 Thought-o-matic

From the book's "see it as an object": give the thought a size, shape, color, speed and texture, and it becomes something you look at instead of from. Build yours:

type a thought above

Answered all five? Now do round two on your reaction to the thought ("I hate that this is here"). Then rebuild the original and check: same size, same color, same speed? It usually isn't.

🫠 Word melter

Two techniques in one: rapid repetition until a harsh word turns to noise (the book uses it on self-labels like "bad parent"), and reversal ("Задом наперед"). Type the word that bites:

the word will fill this space, faster and faster, then flip backwards...

Say it out loud along with the screen if you're alone. Around thirty seconds in, meaning falls off and only sound is left. That's all it ever was: sound, plus your history.

Where these come from Leaves on a stream and passengers on the bus are canonical ACT exercises referenced throughout the book (chapters 9, 12, 15). The object builder is chapter 9's "see it as an object". The melter combines chapter 9's word reversal with the rapid-repeat practice from the stress section of chapter 15.
