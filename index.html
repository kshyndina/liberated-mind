Psychological flexibility, walked through step by step

An interactive study companion to Steven C. Hayes, A Liberated Mind (ACT — Acceptance and Commitment Therapy). Every exercise, self-test, and application here is drawn from the book and rewritten as a walkthrough you can actually do.

The idea in one paragraph

Hayes argues that most psychological suffering comes from rigidity: fusing with our thoughts, avoiding our feelings, running on autopilot, defending a story of who we are, losing contact with what we care about, and acting on impulse or habit instead of choice. The voice driving all of this he calls the Inner Dictator: the problem-solving mind applied to places where problem-solving backfires. The way out is six learnable skills, which he calls pivots. Each pivot takes the energy inside a healthy yearning that has been redirected into a rigid habit and turns it back toward openness.

How to practice (the book's own plan)

  1. Weeks 1–2: work through the pivots in order and do each chapter's starter exercises at least once a day. When a thought hooks you during the day, use one or two on the spot.
  2. After the first pass: come back for the additional exercises and try them all.
  3. Then build a toolkit: pick the exercises that fit you and practice only those for a few months, until they are automatic enough to use anywhere. Add new ones one at a time; drop what doesn't work. That page is My toolkit.
  4. Keep going for life. Fusion never retires; the goal is progress, not perfection. Keep finding new areas to apply the skills to: do one new thing daily that requires openness, and one kind thing daily for someone else.
Skills work as a block The six skills reinforce each other. During any hard piece of work: defusion handles judgmental thoughts about your progress, self keeps you acting from meaning rather than social expectation, acceptance carries you when it hurts, presence keeps you on the process instead of the distance to the goal, values remind you why, and action turns it into behavior.

Honest framing This is a self-made study aid, not therapy and not a replacement for the book. The book itself says: for mental health struggles, use these skills alongside professional help, never instead of it. For eating disorders, seek professional treatment at the first signs.
